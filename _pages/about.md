I am **Tong Bao**, a postdoctoral researcher in the **Massive Stars Group at the University of Potsdam**, Germany. My research focuses on **stellar feedback in low-metallicity galaxies**, using nearby systems as laboratories to explore the role of massive stars and compact objects in the evolution of early galaxies. Building on my work on X-ray source populations and compact binaries, I use X-ray and multiwavelength observations to investigate the high-energy emission from massive stars, accreting binaries, and hot gas, and its implications for stellar feedback.

## Research interests

**Massive stars and high-energy feedback.** I am extending this work to massive stars and low-metallicity star-forming galaxies, investigating the roles of stellar winds, hot gas, and compact sources in their X-ray emission. My broader interests include unusual transients and variability across the high-energy sky.

**Compact binaries and their environments.** Periodic X-ray signals provide clues to the nature of accreting binaries. I search for these signals in the Galactic bulge and globular clusters, combining timing and spectral analysis to study cataclysmic variables and explore how dense stellar environments shape binary evolution.

**The stellar populations behind the Galactic X-ray glow.** I combine X-ray surveys with Gaia observations to distinguish active stars, massive stars, and accreting compact objects. A central aim is to determine which populations produce the Galactic ridge X-ray emission, including its prominent iron lines.

## Selected research

- **An overlooked contribution to Galactic iron-line emission.** We identified a population of X-ray-luminous late-type giants whose hard spectra and strong iron emission point to accretion-powered binaries, highlighting their contribution to the Galactic ridge. [Bao et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09567)

- **Identifying the soft X-ray population of the inner Galactic disk.** By combining eROSITA detections, deeper XMM-Newton observations, and Gaia data, we characterized the stellar and compact-source populations and assessed their contribution to the soft Galactic ridge emission. [Bao et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23814)

- **Using periodic sources to probe binary evolution in globular clusters.** Our Chandra survey identified periodic cataclysmic variables and used their period distribution to investigate the effects of stellar encounters on binary formation and evolution. [Bao et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.14511)
